Hear-a-Note for Toddlers

Short play, real learning.

Early perfect-pitch training and first piano lessons for toddlers 18–36 months, on your real piano

Hear-a-Note for Toddlers listens to your acoustic or digital piano through the microphone. With a grown-up beside them, babies and toddlers aged 18–36 months see a note on the staff, find it on the real keys and hear right away if it was right. Four things make it different: a no-overstimulation design, an early absolute-pitch foundation, natural first sight-reading on the staff, and real-piano play-along. No piano? The on-screen keys work too.

Real-piano play-along

Most music apps keep children tapping glass. Hear-a-Note for Toddlers sends them to the real instrument: the tablet sits on the music stand, shows the note and listens. When your child plays the right key on the piano, the note lights up and soft confetti appears; a wrong key just gets a quiet “mew?”.

  1. Open the app and put the phone or tablet on the piano’s music stand.
  2. In the menu, choose “Play a real piano” and allow the microphone.
  3. Pick a song or the Challenge: the card shows the note on the staff and the shape to look for.
  4. Your child plays it on the real piano — the app hears it, shows the answer and moves on.

Works with acoustic pianos and digital keyboards. Best in a quiet room, one key at a time. The sound is analyzed on the device and never recorded or uploaded.

Four things that make Hear-a-Note for Toddlers different

  • No-overstimulation design: soft colors and gentle sounds, no flashing effects, no ads, no endless levels, no reward loops and no autoplay feed. Made for short, happy moments with a grown-up — not for screen time for its own sake.
  • Early absolute-pitch foundation: every key plays a true, precisely tuned piano note (standard A = 440 Hz), so your baby hears exactly the same pitch every time — a solid first step for ear training and early absolute-pitch practice.
  • Natural first sight-reading on the staff: every note appears on a real treble or bass clef staff, together with a shape and color your baby already knows. No reading needed — children naturally link the note they see to the key they play.
  • Real-piano play-along: the tablet sits on the music stand and listens through the microphone. When your child plays the right key on an acoustic piano or digital keyboard, the note lights up and soft confetti appears — little hands stay on real keys, not on glass.

What is Hear-a-Note for Toddlers?

Hear-a-Note for Toddlers is a free web app that introduces babies and toddlers aged 18–36 months to the piano and to reading music. Every white key shows one big shape and color, and the note card above the keys shows the same note on a real staff. By matching the staff note to the shape and the key, children connect what they see, hear and play — long before they can read words. Like every app in the MyTeachers123.com series, Hear-a-Note for Toddlers is not built around screen fun: it is built to work with the real world — here, the real piano in your home.

For parents of babies and toddlers aged 18–36 months

  • Made for little hands: fullscreen, big keys and no words on the keys — only shapes and colors toddlers already know.
  • Listen first, then play: pick a song and the app plays it slowly while each key lights up; then your child plays along.
  • Gentle feedback: a right note brings soft confetti, a wrong note is a quiet kitten “mew” — never scolding.
  • Safe by design: no ads, nothing for children to tap by accident, a volume limit for little ears, and menus that fold away while your child plays.
  • Grows with your child: when they are ready, the Challenge asks them to read all 8 notes of a clef on their own.
  • Toddler-proof: fullscreen and locked to landscape; the menus fold away and only open with a grown-up’s double-tap, so little fingers do not tap their way out. For a complete lock, turn on Guided Access (iPhone, iPad) or screen pinning (Android).

  • Real-piano mode listens to an acoustic or digital piano through the device microphone, so students answer on the instrument.

How it works

Frequently asked questions

What age is Hear-a-Note for Toddlers for?

It is designed for babies and toddlers aged 18–36 months (1.5 to 3 years), played together with a parent or caregiver. The keys use shapes and colors instead of letters, so children do not need to read. Older children can keep going with the note-reading Challenge.

Is it really free?

Yes. It is free to use, with no ads, no in-app purchases and no sign-up.

Does it collect data about my child?

No personal data is collected. Settings such as the language are saved only on your device. In real-piano mode the microphone sound is analyzed on the device to recognize notes and is not uploaded.

Do we need a real piano?

No. Children play the keys on the screen. If you have a piano or keyboard at home, real-piano mode lets them play on it instead.

Which devices does it work on?

Any modern web browser on phones, tablets and computers, including iPhone, iPad and Android. You can add it to the home screen and use it offline.

Can it help my child learn to read music?

It is a playful first step: children see each note on a real treble or bass clef staff and connect it with a shape, a color, a key and a sound.

How can piano teachers use it?

As a warm-up, as a first introduction to the staff and clefs, or as short home practice. Real-piano mode lets students answer on the instrument.
